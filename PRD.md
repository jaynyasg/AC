### Spring A/C Tune-Up

Preparing your cooling system for the heat of the summer. We will:

- Check refrigerant levels and system pressures
- Inspect condenser coil and clean if necessary
- Test compressor and capacitor operation
- Inspect electrical components and tighten connections
- Check thermostat operation and calibration
- Inspect condensate drain and flush if needed
- Check air filter condition
- Measure temperature split across the coil
- Inspect outdoor unit for proper airflow

### Fall Heating Tune-Up

Make sure your heating system is safe and ready for winter. We will:

- Inspect heat exchanger for cracks or damage
- Test safety controls
- Check gas pressure and burner operation
- Inspect ignition system
- Test blower motor and airflow
- Check thermostat operation
- Inspect flue and venting
- Inspect electrical connections
- Replace or inspect air filter

## FAQ and Homeowner Tips

In order to keep your unit in good shape throughout the year, there are a few tips that are useful as a homeowner to understand that can create longevity and negate certain issues from occurring.

During our preventative maintenance program checks, components that are dangerous for a homeowner to try and mess with are addressed. There are other things as a homeowner that you should feel comfortable with maintaining that don't require professional assistance with executing.

1. You should regularly check filters and replace them every 3 months so you don't add stress to some of these important components. Proper airflow is important in not causing stresses to your overall system performance.
2. Every 6 months, you can typically add a cup of bleach mixed with a gallon of hot water into your "primary drainline" in order to prevent buildup in the pipe so you don't have drainage issues.
3. A very important key practice to be aware of is making sure to turn off your AC during severe thunderstorms. A surge in power, or if a loss of power cuts your system off abruptly, can damage the most expensive components dramatically and may force you into spending thousands to get your system operating again.
4. Try not to add any plants, trees, or have anything obstructing airflow for your outdoor condenser unit in order for optimal performance. Generally, keep a 2-foot boundary in mind.
5. If you have a dog, make sure they don't pee on the outdoor unit. This tends to become a natural behavior that could potentially deteriorate the coil and will shorten the lifespan by creating a refrigerant leak.
